National Heritage Conservation — Revitalizing Ghana’s Modernist & Vernacular Architecture.

Project Feature: National Heritage Conservation

Locations: Accra, Cape Coast, Elmina, Kumasi  |  Focus: Adaptive Reuse, Civic Memory & Vernacular Preservation

Ghana possesses an extraordinary architectural tapestry that bridges pre-colonial indigenous earth architecture, colonial coastal fortifications, and an internationally renowned collection of post-independence modernist civic architecture. The National Heritage Conservation initiative is an interdisciplinary effort led by ASG fellows, university researchers, and public heritage bodies to safeguard these irreplaceable structures.

Digital Documentation & Preservation Technologies

Utilizing high-precision LiDAR scanning, drone photogrammetry, and architectural archival research, the initiative is producing Ghana’s first open-access digital heritage inventory. This repository records structural dimensions, material degradation patterns, and historical significance, enabling architects to propose faithful conservation plans and sensitive modern upgrades.

Key Conservation Sites

Independence Modernism (Accra)

Preservation guidelines for Ghana's post-1957 civic monuments, institutional buildings, and public plazas built during the early republic.

Asante Traditional Buildings

Restoration of UNESCO-listed vernacular earth and timber shrines in the Ashanti Region with traditional master builders.

Coastal Fortresses & Harbours

Structural consolidation against coastal salt-air corrosion, adaptive reuse for civic cultural tourism, and educational public facilities.
